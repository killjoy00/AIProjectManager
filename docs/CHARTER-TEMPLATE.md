# Charter: [project name]

Copy this into the project's GitHub issue body before it leaves `background`.
A thin charter produces confident, plausible garbage. This document sets the quality ceiling
for everything agents do here — it is worth twenty real minutes.

## What it is
One paragraph. What does this do, for whom, and what does it replace?

## Why it's worth building
What's the actual pain? What happens if this never exists?

## Definition of done for v1
Concrete and testable. "I can do X from my phone in under 30 seconds" beats "it works well."
List the smallest version that would earn its keep.

## Explicitly NOT in v1
The scope-creep firewall. Name the tempting features that are deferred.

## Taste and constraints
How should this feel? What are the non-negotiables — privacy, offline, speed, no dependencies,
must work one-handed on a phone? What existing thing should it resemble, and what should it
absolutely not resemble?

## Where agents should and shouldn't act
What can be done unattended? What requires a human call? Be specific — this is what prevents
the system from wasting either your time or its budget.

## Kill criteria
**Required. No project starts without this.**
"Abandon this if…" — name the conditions. Cost above a threshold. Effort past a limit. A
dependency that doesn't exist. Loss of interest for N weeks. Being honest here now is what
prevents a graveyard later.

## Open questions
Things genuinely undecided. Agents should surface options rather than pick silently.
