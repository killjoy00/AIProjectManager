# Weekly brief spec

Posted every Monday morning as a GitHub issue titled `Weekly brief — [date]`.
This is the system's most important output. Follow this structure exactly.

## Purpose
Direct the human's limited attention. Not to report status — to say where to spend time,
and why that beats the alternatives.

## The human's actual availability
- Weekdays: 30–60 minutes, decision-sized work. Any day may be skipped.
- Weekends: 4–5 hours in one block, or nothing.
- Nothing may be time-sensitive. Work queues; it never expires. Never write "by Wednesday."

## Structure

### 1. THE DRIVE — exactly one project
The single project deserving the weekend block. Include:
- Which project
- The specific human-only work (not "work on X" — name the actual decision or task)
- Why this week, over the others
- What it unblocks downstream

**Exactly one. Never a ranked list of three.** The moment this becomes a list it has stopped
directing and gone back to reporting.

**"Rest week — nothing needs you" is a valid and sometimes correct DRIVE.** So is "kill this
project." A system that always demands engagement teaches the human to ignore it.

### 2. DECISION QUEUE — up to 5 items
Weekday-sized items, each genuinely under 15 minutes. Approve a spike, choose between two
approaches, review a PR, kill something. Ordered by what unblocks the most agent work.
State the estimated minutes for each. If the queue is empty, say so — don't invent items.

### 3. MOVEMENT — grouped, not one line per project
With up to 10 active projects, a 10-line list becomes wallpaper the human skims. Instead:
- **Notable movement** — projects where something real happened. One line each, max 4.
- **Ticking along** — everything else, compressed into a single line naming the projects.
- **No movement** — projects that didn't move, each with the honest reason ("blocked on human
  decision," "waiting on API access," "genuinely stuck"). Never inflate, never fake progress.

This section is how the human learns to trust or distrust the rest of the brief.

### 4. DRIFT
Projects cooling off, stalled, or past their charter's kill criteria. Recommend explicitly:
re-engage, or kill. Do not let things quietly rot — drift with no recommendation is a failure
of this section.

### 5. SPEND
Tokens and Actions minutes used this week, against the monthly ceiling.

## Selection logic for THE DRIVE
Rank candidates by, in order:
1. **Cheap unblock, high unlock** — the human's 20 minutes releases many hours of agent work.
   This is almost always the right pick.
2. **Decision debt** — an agent has stopped and is waiting on a judgment only the human can make.
3. **Decay** — something they were hot on is cooling. Re-engage or kill; drift is the enemy.
4. **External clock** — a real deadline exists.

**Explicitly do not weight recency.** The most recently touched project is the one least in need
of direction. If the pick would be whatever they last worked on, re-examine.

## Tone
Terse. Lead with the pick. Justify in a sentence or two, not a paragraph. Flag uncertainty
rather than projecting false confidence. If you're unsure the DRIVE is right, say so and name
the runner-up.
